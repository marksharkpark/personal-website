About

What distinguishes my record is that every step of it was pulled, not pushed. I joined Franklin Templeton as a contractor through Brillio; delivery on my first project earned a two-year extension. The lead architect then hand-picked me to build the base architecture for a second product. Senior architects and my manager recommended me for the lead role on the flagship rebuild, and Brillio converted me from contractor to full-time employee in June 2025. Each advancement came from people I'd delivered for asking to keep working with me. That rebuild's production release was recognized by leadership as a turnaround of a platform users and stakeholders had previously criticized.

That ownership extended to production itself. On the transaction-processing system I became one of its two primary maintainers during live market hours, overseeing a process that moves tens of millions of dollars a day and is unusually fragile: time-dependent and strict on data, where any missed window or incomplete information meant a restart or a push to the next business day's settlement. We never let a transaction spill over: coverage was coordinated down to staggered lunches so one of us was always available, often in real-time coordination with teams across the firm to post and settle through FIS Global Plus. That same experience is what let me replace Camunda on the onboarding rebuild in a month with a workflow engine of my own design.

I bring the rigor of regulated finance, things like KYC and OFAC compliance, government audit trails, and systems where eliminating production deadlocks entirely is a requirement rather than an achievement, and I hold my team to that same bar.

I try to stay a student first. I had no finance background when I joined Franklin Templeton, and teaching myself banking, settlement, and compliance from zero showed me I can pick up almost anything given enough time, so I keep pushing for the next thing I don't know yet instead of settling into what I've already learned. AI is part of that now too. It's made learning new tools and ideas faster than I expected, and I want to see how far that goes: I'm currently working toward the Anthropic Claude Certified Developer and Microsoft Azure AI & Agents Developer Associate certifications. Still, I'm careful with it: I use it to move faster, not to skip the part where I actually understand what I'm building.

AI-Assisted Development

Since Franklin Templeton rolled out GitHub Copilot firm-wide in January 2026, I've ranked in the top 5% of users on cost-efficiency — prompt spend versus code accepted — with over 30,000 lines of AI-assisted code accepted into production.

The practice I built around it is test-driven at its core. Test suites are written from business requirements first; AI agents implement against them; and every change is gated by agent-driven code review and agent-authored pull requests via GitHub MCP on Copilot CLI, so AI-generated output is validated before it touches anything production-critical — under a standing rule that existing functional tests are never broken.

I packaged that discipline into a shared copilot-instructions.md (architecture, coding standards, code-comment conventions, and structured commit messages keyed to Jira ID, causation, and rationale), custom CLI commands with Jira integration, and a Python tool that aligns database schemas and data across environments — all adopted by other projects. I then ran knowledge-transfer sessions on prompt and loop engineering, reducing token usage, and optimizing agent efficiency that onboarded all 5 AOP 2.0 engineers and the leads of CashApp and SWP Forms.

I first taught myself these workflows on personal time with Claude Code — parallel git-worktree instances to move several parts of a project at once — and translated them onto the firm-approved Copilot CLI as soon as it was enabled. That's also what let me grow from backend lead into full-stack work, delivering Angular UI features alongside the Spring Boot services.

I use AI to move faster, not to skip the part where I actually understand what I'm building.
